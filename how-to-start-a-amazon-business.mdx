Amazon remains the largest online marketplace in the United States, with hundreds of millions of active shoppers. New sellers get access to that audience without building their own storefront from scratch. Fulfillment by Amazon lets sellers outsource storage, packing, and shipping, which lowers the operational burden significantly. Many sellers start part-time while sourcing products, then scale once they find a profitable niche.

## Choosing a Selling Model

Decide between retail arbitrage, wholesale reselling, or private label manufacturing, since each has very different capital and time requirements. Retail arbitrage involves buying discounted products in stores and reselling them on Amazon, requiring the least capital to start. Private label typically requires more upfront investment but builds a more defensible, sellable business over time.

## Researching Product Demand

Use Amazon's own best-seller rankings to estimate demand for a product category. A research tool like Jungle Scout or Helium 10 helps too. Look for products with steady demand, manageable competition, and room for a differentiated listing. Avoid categories dominated by major brands unless you have a genuine differentiation angle. A private label improvement in quality or packaging counts.

Checking review counts and complaint patterns on competing listings can reveal exactly what improvement would win over existing customers. Ordering a handful of competitor products yourself is also worth the small expense. Firsthand experience often surfaces issues that reviews alone never mention.

## Registering the Business and Getting an EIN

Forming an LLC is common among serious Amazon sellers, both for liability protection and for a more professional seller account. Register your LLC with your state and apply for a free EIN from the IRS. Amazon requires this EIN for a professional seller account setup. It also lets you separate business expenses cleanly for tax purposes.

Product liability exposure is real for physical goods sold at scale. Many sellers also purchase a separate product liability policy once Amazon requires proof of insurance above certain sales thresholds.

## Licensing, Permits, and Amazon Approval

Most product categories on Amazon require no special government license. Certain categories like supplements, cosmetics, and electronics require Amazon category approval. Check your state for a reseller's permit or sales tax license. You may need to collect and remit sales tax depending on your state and Amazon's marketplace facilitator rules. Private label sellers importing goods also need an importer of record setup and should understand basic customs requirements.

Gated categories can take several weeks for approval. Applying early avoids delaying your product launch once inventory is ready to ship.

## Startup Costs by Selling Model

Retail arbitrage can start with as little as a few hundred dollars in initial inventory. Private label products typically require $2,000 to $10,000 for a first manufacturing order. The exact amount depends on minimum order quantities. Budget separately for Amazon's referral fees, FBA fulfillment fees, and initial advertising spend to launch a new listing. Ranking a new product usually requires some paid traffic.

Product photography and packaging design are additional costs that meaningfully affect conversion rate for private label sellers. Sample orders from a few manufacturers add a modest but necessary cost. Shipping samples to yourself for quality checks is worth it before a full production run. Wholesale sellers should also budget for the minimum purchase requirements many brands set for approved resellers.

## Funding Your Inventory

Many sellers self-fund their first inventory order using personal savings before reinvesting profits into larger orders. Amazon offers seller lending programs to established sellers with consistent sales history. Inventory financing companies specialize in funding purchase orders for growing e-commerce sellers. This helps you scale beyond your own cash flow.

## Pricing for Profit

Price your product by calculating landed cost, Amazon referral fees, FBA fulfillment fees, and your target margin. Research the top-ranking competitor listings in your category to understand the going price range. Building in room for periodic promotions and coupons helps improve conversion rate without eroding your baseline price too much.

Factor in seasonal advertising cost increases too, since PPC bids often rise sharply around major shopping events. This can compress your margin if your pricing has no cushion.

## Getting Your First Sales

Amazon's own advertising platform, Amazon PPC, is the primary channel for reaching buyers early on. Optimizing your title, images, and bullet points matters just as much. Both shoppers and Amazon's search algorithm read them closely. Driving external traffic from social media or email lists can also boost your listing's organic ranking over time.

An early review strategy matters too. Amazon's Vine program helps a new listing gather its first honest reviews. A well-timed follow-up email sequence works faster than organic traffic alone.

## Managing Inventory and Fulfillment

Deciding between Fulfillment by Amazon and fulfilling orders yourself affects both cost and control. FBA handles storage, packing, and shipping, but charges fees that scale with size and weight. Fulfillment by Merchant keeps more margin per sale but requires you to manage returns and shipping speed yourself.

Forecasting demand accurately prevents both stockouts and excess storage fees. Many sellers use a simple spreadsheet tracking sell-through rate before placing reorders. Setting reorder points based on lead time from your manufacturer avoids running out during a sales spike. A stockout during peak season can tank your search ranking for weeks.

## Mistakes New Sellers Make

Underestimating total landed cost, including fees, often turns a seemingly profitable product into a losing one. Ordering too much inventory before validating demand ties up cash in unsold stock. Many new sellers also neglect listing compliance rules, risking suspension for issues like unauthorized claims or missing safety documentation.

Ignoring storage limits and long-term storage fees is another costly oversight. Amazon charges steep penalties for inventory that sits too long in a fulfillment center.

## Expanding Your Product Line

Once your first product is profitable, expand into complementary products within the same customer niche to build a real brand. Hiring a virtual assistant for customer service and listing management frees up time for sourcing new products. Many established sellers eventually build their own Shopify storefront alongside Amazon to reduce platform dependency.

Enrolling in Amazon Brand Registry, once you have a registered trademark, opens up protections against listing hijackers. It also unlocks enhanced content tools for your product pages.
